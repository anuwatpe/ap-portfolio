Academic Profile

From public health practice to biomedical informatics

Three connected areas define the work: representing health knowledge, producing trustworthy evidence, and translating data into practical population health decisions.

Biomedical Informatics & Ontology

Ph.D. and M.S. training in biomedical informatics, with research in formal ontology, semantic integration, and health data.

Evidence Synthesis & Biostatistics

Systematic reviews, meta-analyses, research appraisal, and quantitative methods grounded in public health practice.

Digital & Population Health

Health information systems, epidemiological informatics, and practical digital tools for communities and healthcare teams.

Teaching, mentorship & professional service

Teaching and supervising students, sharing expertise through invited talks, and contributing to peer review and the biomedical ontology community.
